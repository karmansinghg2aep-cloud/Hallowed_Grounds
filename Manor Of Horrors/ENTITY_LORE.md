# Hallowed Grounds: entity field guide

## The Program

The five tapes are an intake-and-copying procedure disguised as an escape test. The player is a living subject whose memories are being recorded to reconstruct a missing person. The Guide is the recording assembled from subjects who chose to stay; its calm instructions are an attempt to keep the player company, shaped by the Program into compliance language. The five Designations are not species. They are escape responses preserved from earlier subjects and split apart so they appear unrelated.

## Designations

| Entity | What it is | Appearance and behavior | Player response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 — The Walker** | A subject's repeated flight response, trapped in its last route. | A blank figure in a grey coverall. It walks a fixed path without adapting. When close, pinhole eyes appear and follow the player. | Learn its route and pass behind it. It can find a player who stays in a footage gap too long. |
| **02 — The Drop** | The instant a subject loses their footing, preserved as a falling frame. | A person-shaped tear in the image, made of scanlines. It patrols and floats. The dive follows the last recorded position, then it rises and resets. |
| **03 — The Listener** | A subject's freeze response, misfiled as an inert object. | A folded, nearly motionless body with its face tucked down. Proximity wakes it; once awake it unfolds and pursues. | Keep distance. It does not need sound or light to find you. A footage gap can break its pursuit. |
| **04 — The Witness** | The act of watching the room, separated from the person doing it. | A stationary recorder-like figure that turns its attention toward movement and fires along its line of sight. | Break alignment, use platforms, and avoid crossing its view. |
| **05 — The Copy** | The Program's incomplete reconstruction of the current subject. | It resembles the game's familiar forms but has a thin pale ring and unnaturally clean edges. It is assembled from missing frames, so its movements feel slightly wrong. | Treat its warning and movement as real even when its appearance resembles another Designation. |

## The five recorded presences

### The Glitch — Doorstalker

Elias Venn was the manor's carpenter. After a flood, he sealed the north wing and reported knocks from inside the walls. During the reconstruction tests, every door in that wing began opening onto the same impossible corridor. Venn disappeared. His apron was found nailed to a door with no hinges.

The Glitch wears the outline of a door panel, with brass hinge joints where its elbows should be. It appears in a lit doorway, rushes through the subject's last position, then appears in another doorway and rushes again toward the subject's new position. Both passes are announced by a bright frame and an alarm. After the second crash, its keyhole opens briefly. Touch it with the charge during that pause.

### The Signal — Projector

Projectionist Mara Doss was hired to restore the manor's fire-drill reel. Each repaired copy placed her eleven seconds later in the booth, until she vanished from the room and remained only on the film. The last frame shows a figure standing behind the camera, though the camera was bolted against a wall.

The Signal is a floor projector on a tripod, with two reels that turn at different speeds and a strip of film leaking from its case. It sweeps one bright beam down the room. Leave the stripe; when the machine stops and its lens turns pale, touch it with the charge.

### The Counsel — former attorney

Ada Mercer was the manor's attorney. She represented residents whose names vanished from the house records and appealed each disappearance. She died at the bench during her final case. The next day, a new argument appeared in the transcript, signed by every missing resident.

Her body remains draped in a ruined court robe, with a powdered wig, cracked scales, and a gavel grown into one arm. The Counsel stamps a red seal beneath the subject's feet. The gavel strikes that spot after a clear warning. Every third ruling, she instead fires a three-paper spread after marked trajectory warnings. Dodge the seal or spread, then approach while she recovers. No extra enemies or hidden counters are involved.

### The Gate — former night porter

Hollis Reed worked the night desk and guarded a west exit that had already been bricked up. His shift book lists everyone he turned away. It also lists Hollis, though nobody remembers seeing him leave the desk.

The Gate is an iron exit folded into a walking body on jointed struts. It raises its crown, slams the floor, and sends a shockwave along the ground. Jump over the wave, then stomp the opened crown. At two plates, it slams twice, sending a second, faster wave before its crown stays open.

### The Record — formerly called the Warden

Nell Rusk kept the Program's ledger. She marked each subject RECOVERED or NOT RECOVERED, never by name. After her death, new entries appeared in her handwriting. Each was signed by a different hand, and every one recorded the same room number.

The Record is a living index card made from erased attempts. Sprocket holes run down its body; a broken timecode circles it like a halo. Avoid its shots. In the final phase, it chases while the arena loses platforms; strike its core only during the brief opening. The fight's central rule stays the same: watch the tell, avoid the marked danger, then use the charge during the opening.

## Footage gaps

The old metal lockers are removed from the fiction. A gap is a place where the recording failed to agree with itself: a narrow tear in the image that temporarily hides the player between frames. The player must stay still to remain there. A nearby Designation can eventually find the gap, and the Auditor cannot verify someone who is inside it.

The ending's **STAY** choice joins the recorded voices in their quiet room and continues the loop. **LET GO** ends the current subject's part in the reconstruction.
